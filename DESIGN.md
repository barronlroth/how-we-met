# Design System: How We Met — Florida V2

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "A Florida Postcard in Motion"**

Florida is a playful miniature waterfront with five distinct districts: the urban New River, a superyacht marina, a shaded mangrove cut, a broad party cove, and the bridge approach. Restaurants, working boatyards, rooted banks, rafted boats, and a crowded Fisheries terrace frame the detailed cream, coral, and teal airboat and rounded couple. Blender-authored residences, hotels, clubs, and towers add recessed facades, balconies, pools, and planted terraces. Close bank planting, second rows of homes, and layered forest and skyline fill the view beyond the waterfront. The route compresses and rearranges Fort Lauderdale references for racing; it is not a literal geographic route. Smaller sculpted heads, broad cheek and jaw planes, painted facial expression, tapered limbs and hands, directional dark curls, and broad golden blonde waves carry the selected animated-feature direction. Nina’s blonde hair follows the user’s correction and supersedes the brown hair in the selected illustration. Sparse arcade instruments leave the course and couple primary.

This record captures the built Florida V2 world, its keyboard and touch instruments, and its homepage link. The user pinned the original generated concept as the visual target; it remains a reference, not runtime imagery or proof of equivalent fidelity. Toronto retains its pixel art, Press Start 2P, Quicksand, and existing palette.

**Key Characteristics:**

- Shaped hull, modeled deck seams, separate upholstered seats, and an exposed fan and engine.
- Proportioned seated couple, landmark-aligned painted faces and glossy almond eye surfaces, dark curls, blonde waves, and Nina’s pointing pose.
- Five district compositions, changing channel widths and water colors, marina and mangrove branches, 167 physical moorings, and active crossing traffic.
- Six Blender-authored waterfront templates, close bank planting, second-row homes, and inexpensive forest and skyline layers extending beyond the foreground.
- Cream and navy instruments, yellow actions, aqua Cafecito feedback, and visible cyan water shots across keyboard and touch controls.
- Close couple framing at entry and finish, with bridge and Fisheries visible on desktop.
- Safe-area-aware thumb controls, a clear portrait chase view, and native entry/pause settings.
- Brief fictional friend roasts from Nina in quieter racing moments, with checkpoint and collision callouts taking priority.

**The Chapter Boundary Rule.** Extend these tokens within Florida; retain Toronto’s visual system and the homepage link’s Quicksand.

## Colors

The frontmatter owns reusable UI values; `scripts/build-florida-hero.py` owns the hero’s authored materials, with exposed skin colors taken from `art/characters/skin-palette-v4.json`, while `scripts/build-florida-waterfront.py` owns the waterfront kit’s twelve opaque PBR materials. `materials.js`, `art.js`, `detail-art.js`, `district-art.js`, and `world.js` own the other scene colors. Sidecar tonal ramps are derived panel previews, not runtime tokens.

- **Primary:** warm yellow identifies actions, focus, gold targets, best times, and race callouts. Aqua fills the keyboard Cafecito gauge; that arc turns pale cream during boost. Touch Cafecito starts yellow and turns aqua while held or boosting, with a navy fuel strip. Held Drift controls invert to cream with navy text; held or firing SOAK uses aqua with navy text; the steering pad uses aqua for held steering and yellow for braking.
- **Secondary:** coral marks the map position and impact frame. Pink distinguishes Flamingo protection; Nina’s speaker label is coral.
- **Neutral:** cream text sits against translucent navy instruments and panels. Silver and bronze accompany written medal labels. The homepage link keeps its separate teal, cream, and yellow values.

**The Moving Background Rule.** Keep the clock and sector label on their navy plates so the animated world cannot erase them.

## Typography

Bungee is declared at weight 400; Nunito’s regular and bold files serve the CSS weight ranges. Timers use tabular figures. The desktop race clock is 37px, position 52px, speed 38px, result time 43px, result title 25px, and centered dialog title 27px. The title wraps at 1000px and below. The homepage chapter title remains Quicksand.

Touch keeps the same families and hierarchy: instructions use Nunito at 14px, dropping to 12px in the smallest portrait and short landscape layouts. Repeated settings labels and touch action labels use the frontmatter’s 11px roles; native selected values use 13px. All newly added touch instructions and functional labels stay at or above 11px. Portrait captions use 12px and short landscape captions use 11px. The touch title is 36px at widths of 600px and below, 31px at 360px and below, and 32px in short landscape. Smaller type is reserved for supporting instruments, with the driving controls retaining written action labels.

**The Instrument Type Rule.** Use Bungee for short race titles and numbers; use Nunito for sentences, controls, and captions.

**The Clear Course Rule.** Place touch controls in the safe-area-adjusted lower corners and keep race information above or between them, preserving the forward route in portrait and landscape.

## Elevation & Depth

The world uses cast shadows, warm directional light, hemisphere fill, atmospheric fog, reflective Three.js water, and nearby screen-space contact shading. The hero’s authored PBR materials distinguish warm skin, hair, upholstery, rubber, metal, and painted hull. One compact facial atlas supplies albedo to the sculpted faces and shallow glossy eye lenses; exposed skin materials are sampled per character from non-blushed regions of the original atlas. A warm, shadow-free point light follows the staged camera only at entry and finish (intensity 90, range 24m); it is hidden during racing. Its golden deck uses solid material colors with modeled caulking, staggered joints, and fasteners. Generated teak texture remains on yachts and docks. Generic scene materials use smooth shading with roughness 0.62; the hero and waterfront material sets vary by surface. Preserve these differences instead of restoring V1’s blanket flat shading.

Water combines seeded periodic normal detail, broad color variation, reflections, and localized crest highlights, with quieter white crests around the foreground boat. Its tint eases from river teal and marina blue-green into green mangroves, bright turquoise in the cove, and blue-green at the bridge. The wake is broken, fading foam with transparent edges and spray; it trails the boat’s actual heading. Restaurant terraces and towers, dense marina slips, layered mangrove roots and canopies, and open sandy party banks provide distinct depths and silhouettes. Reused geometry is instanced and batched into 100m regions for the camera and water reflection. Foreground regions remain visible within 670m of course progress, with contact shading limited to 95m. A separate set of low-cost forest and skyline regions extends to 1700m and skips contact shading and shadow casting/receiving. Three overlapping forest bands and varied distant tower heights fill gaps behind the close architecture. Wider sculpted cumulus banks have baked vertex-color undersides; a soft sun glow and atmospheric fog connect them to the horizon. Feathered palm leaflets leave daylight through their crowns. UI lift is concentrated on the primary action and modal panels; the touch steering knob adds a small downward shadow to distinguish its movable surface. Exact shadows are in the sidecar.

Smooth and Detailed preserve the same geometry, textures, water, lights, and art authority. Smooth caps DPR at 1, uses two-sample anti-aliasing and 1024px shadows, and omits screen-space ambient occlusion. Detailed retains the previous 1.25 DPR cap, four samples, 2048px shadows, and ambient occlusion. These profiles alter rendering cost and contact depth, with performance evidence scoped to the tested host and viewport.

The camera eases into a 63° staged view, 68° race view, or 84° boosted view. Steering banks the hull; speed adds slight vibration and peripheral streaks. Reduced motion removes CSS transitions, streaks, flash, camera vibration/shake, boost FOV expansion, hull bob/roll/pitch, and Nina’s body roll. Water, fan, wakes, pickups, some character motion, and camera following continue.

## Shapes

The airboat has a curved, beveled hull, thin rails, separate seats, and a detailed coral fan cage. Each character has a smaller head seated lower into the neck, a shorter lower face, wider cheek/jaw planes, and integrated nose, brow, socket and muzzle relief. Near-uniform UV mapping aligns the approved facial albedo with these landmarks instead of stretching the nose-to-mouth span. The albedo carries brows, green or hazel eyes, smiles, and Nina’s visible teeth; convex almond eye lenses follow the same landmarks and add gloss. The painted expressions are fixed, with no facial animation rig.

Barron’s dark hair combines a shaped hairline and crown with directional curled locks. Nina’s golden blonde hair combines a rounded mantle with broad, asymmetric, tapered waves; lighter locks follow the volume instead of separate raised stripes. Barron wears the selected sage linen shirt, teal shorts, chain, and stud; Nina wears the coral crew-neck T-shirt, cream shorts, and hoops. Shirts merge shoulder and sleeve volumes into continuous cloth; shorts merge the hips and seated thighs. Tapered limbs lead into palms, thumbs, and fingers. The articulated pointing arm preserves the passenger pose.

The six-root Blender kit adds a staggered waterfront residence with pool and pergola, a rounded marina hotel with balcony ribbons, a stepped skyline tower, and an open dining club with rooftop terrace. Its simpler skyline and canopy templates form the distant layers. Architecture combines recessed balconies, pitched roofs, flat terraces, and repeated verticals; feathered palms and layered trees keep the waterfront’s miniature scale. New River adds awnings, cafe decks, promenade rails, benches, and a small raised bridge. The marina adds sail masts, rigging, lifting gantries, service sheds, and a yacht club. Mangroves use curved roots and branches beneath layered solid canopies, with boardwalks and pelicans. The cove adds sand, parasols, bar decks, beached pontoons on slipways, and rafted boats. The finale adds waterfront terraces and a fuller Fisheries deck while preserving the large bridge silhouette. UI uses small keycaps, rounded tools and actions, wider panel corners, and pill medals. Outline icons and the semicircular keyboard coffee gauge retain an arcade instrument character. Touch extends that vocabulary with an oval steering pad, a circular movable knob, and broad rounded action surfaces. Drift/SOAK/Cafecito share the touch-action radius, switching to the compact radius in short landscape; native settings fields use the smaller field radius.

**The Rounded Couple Rule.** Preserve the approved facial atlas, its landmark alignment, proportional heads and bodies, Barron’s dark curls, Nina’s golden blonde waves, selected outfits, and readable seated poses when extending the couple’s art.

## Components

- **Actions and tools:** yellow primary controls have a 180px minimum width on desktop, 2px hover lift and 2px pressed offset; disabled controls reduce opacity. Navy sound/pause controls include outline icons. Topbar controls and entry/dialog text actions retain at least 44px hit heights; icon-only topbar controls also retain a 44px minimum width. Text actions underline on hover. Buttons and links use a 3px yellow focus ring with a 5px offset.
- **Race instruments:** short labels, tabular timing, rank out of four, real course progress, and a curved Cafecito meter in keyboard mode. Touch keeps rank/speed above the thumb groups and integrates fuel into Cafecito; SOAK carries a thin refill strip and firing color instead of a duplicate HUD chip. Keyboard mode shows SOAK with a Space keycap. Item chips show Flamingo protection and SPF time. Drift, slipstream, close-call, and boost callouts occupy the left side.
- **Water blaster:** a compact cream, teal, and yellow deck cannon fires cyan droplets with pale cores along the boat’s heading. Hold Space or SOAK to repeat shots; steering aims subsequent shots. A first impact produces spray and an expanding water ring. A struck tube rider moves aside, a gator ducks, or a rival boat rocks and slows for 1.8 seconds. Rivals recover between effective hits; repeated shots during their 2.8-second protection window still splash but do not stack slowdown. Reduced motion disables the added rival rocking. The cannon and shots add no light or shadow pass.
- **Nina caption:** compact navy backing, a coral speaker label, and a polite live region; the runtime fades short comments. `nina-lines.js` contains 25 fictional roasts, five each for Miles, Brauser, Josh, Clark, and Dewey. Each run offers at most one turn per friend, starting after ten racing seconds and spacing delivered jokes at least thirteen seconds apart. Replays rotate the selection. Checkpoint and collision calls can interrupt banter; jokes wait through those calls and a further 1.5-second gap. Routine reward chatter yields to an active joke. Pausing and finishing do not consume a line, and delayed jokes never burst as a queue.
- **Dialogs:** pause and finish trap Tab navigation, including the native settings selects; resume restores focus and finish focuses replay. Native selects own Enter, Escape, arrows, and editing keys. Buttons and links retain native activation, while Space for water shots and Escape for pause remain available from the canvas. Results show rank/medal, time, browser-local best, jumps, close calls, and bumps. Demo completion uses its own copy and does not save records. Thresholds remain in `MEDAL_TIMES` in `core.js`.
- **Entry and fallback:** Start race and Watch a run share the entry scene; the demo offers Take the wheel. Preserve the device-appropriate control guide, input and graphics selectors, and actionable graphics-failure panel. The homepage chapter link retains Quicksand and its original styling.
- **Touch driving controls:** the pad is 144×120px with a 50px knob; at 360px and below it narrows to 130px. The right group is 164px wide with 64px-minimum Drift/SOAK controls and a 72px-minimum Cafecito control, narrowing to 144px on the smallest portrait layout. Short landscape uses a 100px-tall pad and a 150px-wide right group with 52px/60px minimum action heights. Pointer capture preserves independent fingers through movement outside a target; release, cancellation, or lost capture clears that pointer without dropping the others. Pause, restart, finish, control changes, blur, and rotation clear all held input. A ten-percent steering dead zone and pull-down brake hysteresis stabilize small movements. Cafecito pairs its fuel strip and color states with HOLD TO BOOST, VAMOS!, or DRIFT TO REFILL; SOAK retains its written action label while a thin strip shows the short refill; held/firing states turn aqua. Keyboard steering remains available in touch mode.
- **Settings fields:** entry and pause repeat native Controls and Graphics selects on translucent navy, with cream values, a light border, 44px minimum height, and a 3px yellow focus ring offset by 3px. Both settings apply immediately and persist in the current browser when storage is available. Coarse-pointer capability defaults to Touch/Smooth; hybrid touch detection only changes controls when the player has no explicit choice. An explicit Keyboard selection survives subsequent touches. Smooth and Detailed retain the same driving mechanics and assets.
- **District world:** `course.js` defines five regions and the shared mooring transforms. `world.js` composes the Blender waterfront kit and native Three.js geometry for each region and renders the same 167 mooring hull models, scales, and headings used by collision. More frequent berths, close hedges and palms, and second rows of houses restore occupied banks. Reduced channel widths bring those banks closer; extra space remains around the departing yacht and island splits. A transverse yacht departs its marina berth; the player collides with its full hull, water shots stop at it, and rival boats steer around its predicted position. Staggered tube riders and a taxi make the open cove crossing. Markers identify both island branches, with extra Cafecito on the mangrove cut.
- **Hero airboat and couple:** `hero-art.js` loads the authored `airboat-couple-v5.glb`, preserving separate hull, fan, Barron, Nina, and pointing-arm nodes for existing runtime animation and item states. The nameplate and Flamingo Floatie are added in code. Material merging follows those articulation boundaries.

## Do's and Don'ts

**Do:**

- Do preserve the couple, readable hazards, curved course, and desktop arrival landmarks.
- Do extend the built shaped geometry and material detail when adding Florida scenery.
- Do preserve district-specific silhouettes, open racing lanes, shared hull transforms, and the marina-to-mangrove-to-cove contrast instead of repeating villas along every bank.
- Do retain close shoreline density, second-row homes, overlapping forest, and a cheap continuous horizon when adding or optimizing scenery.
- Do preserve the approved facial atlas and correct its mapping and surrounding geometry when proportions distort the likeness; retain dark curls, blonde waves, selected outfits, and readable pointing pose.
- Do keep the user-approved FLORIDA eyebrow and original concept reference.
- Do pair color-coded states with text or recognizable geometry.
- Do preserve keyboard focus, pause behavior, independent touch pointers, and the touch driving instructions.
- Do retain at least 44px touch targets and 11px newly added touch labels, with written action names and visible firing/refill states.
- Do let native settings own editing keys while keeping Tab inside dialogs and canvas driving shortcuts available.

**Don't:**

- Don’t apply Florida’s fonts, palette, or 3D art rules to Toronto.
- Don’t substitute the concept image for the playable world or a runtime screenshot.
- Don’t confuse the generated facial albedo with Blender-authored geometry, native-canvas materials, supplied portraits, or a concept-image crop.
- Don’t describe desktop viewport checks as physical phone testing or reduced motion as a static scene.
- Don’t let automatic touch detection override an explicit control choice or let held inputs survive pause, rotation, or leaving the app.
- Don’t use long egg-shaped heads, rope-like hair, detached sleeve flanges, or blocky limb ends to carry the selected character art.
- Don’t turn authoring renders, browser stills, or individual benchmarks into claims of concept equality, photorealistic likeness, or universal 60 FPS.
